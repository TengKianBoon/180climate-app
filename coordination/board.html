## The bug — REDD loss uncertainty uses the RAW CV, not the standard error of the mean
`_estimate_redd` sets `CV_loss = std_l / mean_l` (raw inter-annual CV ≈ 0.88 for HTI). The baseline projects the **mean**
loss rate; the uncertainty in a mean is the **standard error = std/√n**, not the raw CV. The raw CV overstates it by √n
(~2.8×) → useless range (HTI **0.73–15.78M, 21×**; `sigma>1` clips the low to 0).

**Fix (one line):** in `_estimate_redd`, use the **standard error of the mean**:
`sigma_loss = (std_l / mean_l) / sqrt(n)`  where `n = len(recent)` (the number of years in the series).
(For n=8, CV 0.877 → **0.31**; combined `sigma = sqrt(0.31² + density_CV²)` ≈ **0.37**; HTI range ≈ **4.6–11.4M** —
wider than the old 5.8–8.3M, reflecting real uncertainty, but **usable**.)
- Keep density CV as-is (ESA 0.20 / IPCC 0.30 — a systematic-uncertainty floor, correctly **not** narrowed by √n).
- Keep the quadrature combine, the separate labelled buffer, and the `max(0, …)` guard (it just won't trigger now).
- Update the `uncertainty` string wording (it's a standard-error-of-the-mean loss CI, not the raw CV).
